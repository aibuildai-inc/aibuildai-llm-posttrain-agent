# Principle 7: Weighted Averaging

**Space**: Prediction-space aggregation.

**Source**: scikit-learn `VotingClassifier` with weights; Caruana et al. 2004, *"Ensemble Selection from Libraries of Models"* (ICML).

## Definition

Aggregate member predictions as `Σ wᵢ · pᵢ` where the weights `wᵢ` are derived from validation performance rather than held equal. The same form covers weighted soft voting (combining probability vectors), weighted rank averaging (combining rank vectors), and weighted regression averaging.

## Mechanism

Weight-selection strategies fall into four families: (a) **grid search** over simple integer weights on out-of-fold predictions; (b) **Caruana-style greedy forward selection** — iteratively add the member that most improves the OOF metric, with replacement, so that the final count of each member acts as its weight; (c) **derivative-free optimizers** (Nelder-Mead, CMA-ES) that minimize OOF loss directly; (d) **linear or ridge regression** of member OOF predictions onto the target, which can produce unconstrained weights. Once weights are fit on OOF, they are applied unchanged at test time.

## Pros

- Automatically downweights weaker members, reducing the penalty of including a bad model in the ensemble.
- Greedy selection optimizes arbitrary non-differentiable metrics (F1, AUC, mean average precision) directly.
- Regression-based weight fitting can discover beneficial de-biasing combinations that equal weighting misses.

## Cons

- The weights are themselves parameters fit on validation data, so they can overfit. Overfitting is worse when the number of members is large relative to the amount of OOF data.
- Unconstrained regression weights can fit noise unless regularized or constrained (non-negative, sum-to-one).
- Requires trustworthy OOF predictions — leakage between training and OOF generation silently poisons the weights.

## When to Use

When members differ meaningfully in quality, when OOF data is abundant enough to stabilize weight fitting, and when the metric is fixed and cheaply computable. Useful as a successor to equal-weight averaging once a clear quality gap between members emerges.

## Common Misconception

That weights must be non-negative and sum to one. Unconstrained linear regression on OOF predictions can yield negative weights that act as **de-biasing correctors**: effectively subtracting one correlated member's bias from another's prediction. Defensible when base members are highly correlated and the regression is regularized; without regularization, negative weights usually overfit noise. Practitioners disagree on whether to allow negative weights; the safe default is non-negative with sum-to-one constraints and escalate only with evidence.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "The predicted class probabilities for each classifier are collected, multiplied by the classifier weight, and averaged. The final class label is then derived from the class label with the highest average probability."
>
> — scikit-learn `VotingClassifier` documentation with `weights` parameter
