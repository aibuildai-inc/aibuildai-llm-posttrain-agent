# Principle 5: Probability Averaging (Soft Voting)

**Space**: Prediction-space aggregation.

**Source**: scikit-learn Ensemble documentation (`VotingClassifier`, `VotingRegressor`).

## Definition

Each of N member models emits a probability distribution over classes (sigmoid for binary or multi-label, softmax for multi-class). The ensemble prediction is the **arithmetic mean of those distributions**, and the final label is the argmax of the averaged distribution. For dense prediction tasks (segmentation, per-pixel classification) the same averaging is applied pixel-wise to probability maps.

## Mechanism

Soft voting preserves each member's confidence. A sample where all members output 0.95 for the positive class aggregates differently from one where all members output 0.51 — after averaging, one is a strong positive, the other a marginal call. The final decision is then a single threshold (typically 0.5, often tuned on held-out data) applied to the averaged probability. In contrast to hard voting, no information about margin or confidence is discarded.

## Pros

- Preserves confidence information, which matters for ties, threshold tuning, and calibration downstream.
- Natural fit for probability-consuming metrics: log-loss, Brier score, AUC.
- Smoothly handles close-call samples where members disagree but one is more confident.

## Cons

- Assumes member probabilities are **on the same scale**. Different loss functions, different temperatures, and different training recipes can produce systematically over- or under-confident outputs; naive averaging biases toward whichever member is sharpest.
- A single badly-calibrated member can dominate the average and pull the ensemble toward its errors.

## When to Use

When member models are structurally similar (same architecture, different seeds or folds) or known to be calibrated (e.g. after temperature scaling on a held-out set). Default choice for soft-target metrics like AUC or log-loss on a homogeneous model family.

## Common Misconception

That soft voting is unconditionally better than hard voting. It is better only when probabilities are calibrated and comparable across members. With uncalibrated members, hard voting or rank averaging frequently matches or beats naive soft voting because they discard the unreliable confidence signal that soft voting trusts.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "Predicts the class label based on the argmax of the sums of the predicted probabilities, which is recommended for an ensemble of well-calibrated classifiers."
>
> — scikit-learn `VotingClassifier` documentation
