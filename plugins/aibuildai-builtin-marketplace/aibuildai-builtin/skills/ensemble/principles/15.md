# Principle 15: Stacking and Blending

**Space**: Model-space / meta.

**Source**: Wolpert 1992, *"Stacked Generalization"*; scikit-learn `StackingClassifier`, `StackingRegressor`; MLWave *"Kaggle Ensembling Guide"* for the stacking/blending distinction.

## Definition

Train M base learners, then train a **meta-learner** on the base learners' predictions to produce the final output. Stacking and blending differ only in **how the meta-training features are generated**.

- **Stacking**: use K-fold cross-validation to produce out-of-fold predictions for every training row. These OOF predictions form the meta-training matrix. The base learners are then refit on the full training set for inference, and the meta-learner predicts on those refit base predictions at test time.
- **Blending**: reserve a single holdout split of the training data (typically 10–15%). Base learners train on the remaining 85–90% and predict once on the holdout, which becomes the meta-training matrix. Less work than stacking, but wastes the holdout portion.

## Mechanism

The meta-learner discovers **which base model to trust in which region** of input space — or, equivalently, learns a correction on top of the base predictions. For stacking to be valid, the OOF predictions must come from models that never saw the row being predicted; violating this requirement leaks information into the meta-features and causes the meta-learner to overfit.

## Pros

- Combines heterogeneous strong base models in a principled, learned way.
- The meta-learner can capture non-uniform regional reliability that equal or weighted averaging cannot.
- Stacking uses all training data efficiently via CV.

## Cons

- **Stacking is expensive**: K× base-model training + 1 refit on full data + 1 meta-training per base family.
- **Blending wastes the holdout portion** for base training, giving noisier meta-features.
- **Leakage is easy and silent.** Any information flow from the row being predicted into the base models' OOF output poisons the meta-learner.

## When to Use

When heterogeneous strong base models have demonstrably uncorrelated errors (check OOF correlation), when data volume is large enough to spare CV folds or a holdout, and when the metric is clearly measurable on OOF. Often the last method to try, after equal and weighted averaging have both been tried.

## Common Misconception

On meta-learner complexity: newcomers often reach for a complex meta-learner (deep network, large tree ensemble). The **community consensus** is the opposite — keep the meta-learner simple: ridge regression, logistic regression, or a small constrained linear blend. Complex meta-learners overfit the narrow OOF feature space and undo the diversity benefit. scikit-learn's defaults reflect this (LogisticRegression / RidgeCV).
