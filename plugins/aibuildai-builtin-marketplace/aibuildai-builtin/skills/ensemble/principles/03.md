# Principle 3: Last-K Checkpoint Averaging

**Space**: Weight-space. Produces a single model.

**Source**: PyTorch `swa_utils` docs; timm `avg_checkpoints.py`; classical Polyak-Ruppert averaging.

## Definition

Save checkpoints at the last K evaluation points of a normal training run (any schedule), then element-wise average their parameter tensors into a single model. No LR modification, no in-training bookkeeping, no retraining — purely a post-hoc operation.

## Mechanism

Any training run that saves checkpoints at regular intervals already holds K parameter vectors. Averaging them element-wise produces a new model whose weights are the arithmetic mean of the K. In the same way SWA benefits from averaging iterates in a flat region, last-K averaging benefits when the last K checkpoints were collected *after* the learning rate had decayed enough that the weights were no longer moving rapidly — i.e., during the tail of a decay schedule. The averaged model is deployed as-is.

## Pros

- Zero training modification. Works on any existing training script with checkpoint saving.
- Single-model inference cost.
- Drop-in retroactive improvement — an already-completed training run can be upgraded by this step alone.

## Cons

- Quality is sensitive to which K checkpoints are included (spacing, training phase). Averaging checkpoints from radically different training stages harms the result.
- No theoretical guarantee of flat-minimum geometry — unlike SWA, the LR schedule is not designed to keep iterates in a flat region, so gains are less consistent.
- BN running statistics in the averaged model may be stale. Recalibrating via a single forward pass over training data (same procedure as SWA) improves results, but practitioners often skip it.

## When to Use

When an existing training run is complete or the LR schedule cannot be modified, and a free post-hoc accuracy upgrade is worth the few minutes of compute. Commonly used as a baseline before deciding whether to invest in proper SWA with a modified schedule.

## Common Misconception

That last-K averaging is identical to SWA. PyTorch documentation is explicit that it is not: SWA additionally requires a modified cyclical or high-constant LR schedule and BN-stat re-estimation. Plain last-K averaging does neither. Last-K is the schedule-agnostic, effort-minimal cousin of SWA, not a synonym.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "Averaged SGD is often employed in conjunction with a decaying learning rate… By contrast, SWA is focused on an equal average of SGD iterates with a modified cyclical or high constant learning rate."
>
> — PyTorch `swa_utils` documentation
