# Principle 11: Multi-Crop Ensemble

**Space**: Data / pipeline.

**Source**: He et al. 2015, *"Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition"* (ResNet, arXiv:1512.03385); Simonyan & Zisserman 2014, VGG §4.

## Definition

From a resized test image, extract **K spatial crops** at fixed positions — classically 10: the four corners, the center, plus horizontal flips of each — forward each crop independently through the model, and average the softmax outputs.

## Mechanism

A classifier trained with random-crop augmentation has learned approximate invariance to spatial position of the object, but the learned invariance is imperfect and a single center crop at test time can miss information from the edges of the image. Multi-crop evaluates the model at K deterministic positions so that the object is guaranteed to be centered in at least one crop, and averages the predictions so that the ensemble is less sensitive to crop position. The 10-crop protocol became a de facto standard on ImageNet classification benchmarks.

## Pros

- **Well-understood, standard** protocol with decades of benchmarking history.
- **Complementary to multi-scale** evaluation: crops handle boundary regions differently from dense or scale-varied evaluation.
- No retraining required.

## Cons

- Inference cost multiplies by K (typically 10).
- Assumes the label can be inferred from each crop individually. Small objects near image boundaries may fall outside most crops and be under-represented.
- **Inappropriate for segmentation or detection** at native resolution, where global spatial context is part of the task.
- Modern classifiers with global average pooling often close most of the gap between single center-crop and 10-crop, reducing the marginal benefit.

## When to Use

Classification of centered objects when the last 0.1–0.5% of accuracy matters and K× inference latency is acceptable. Classic use case: ImageNet-style single-label classification benchmarks. Less useful for modern GAP-based classifiers and inappropriate for dense prediction.

## Common Misconception

That 10-crop is the same as multi-scale. It is **single-scale with spatial variation** — all crops are at the same resolution. Multi-scale varies the input resolution. The two techniques are complementary, and the original VGG paper advocates combining them, but they are not synonyms.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "Multi-crop evaluation performs slightly better than dense evaluation, and the two approaches are indeed complementary."
>
> — Simonyan & Zisserman 2014, VGG §4
