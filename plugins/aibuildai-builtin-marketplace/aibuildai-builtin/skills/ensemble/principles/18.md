# Principle 18: How to Combine Methods Across Spaces

**Cross-cutting.** Because the four ensemble spaces introduce diversity at different stages — training-time parameter averaging, test-time prediction aggregation, input variation, and architectural variation — they **compose multiplicatively**. A mature pipeline applies one technique from each space, stacking their gains without conflict.

## The Composition Mental Model

```
Per-design training pipeline:
  weight-space method            (SWA or EMA — 0 inference overhead)
    × data-pipeline CV ensemble  (K fold models — K× inference)
      × data-pipeline TTA        (N augmentations per forward — N× inference)

Across designs:
  model-space cross-architecture aggregator
    (soft-prob or rank average; optionally weighted; optionally stacked with ridge meta-learner)
```

The total inference cost is the **product of the multipliers** at each layer: K × N × M architectures forwards per test sample, plus aggregation. Storage is the sum of all saved checkpoints. Training cost is the sum of all pipeline trainings; weight-space methods contribute zero additional inference cost but may modify the LR schedule of their host training run.

## Sequencing the Methods

The conventional order of introduction, from most cost-effective to most expensive, is:

1. **Within each member's training**: add EMA (zero-cost, schedule-agnostic) or SWA (small schedule modification).
2. **Within each member's inference**: add TTA with a validated set of augmentations.
3. **Across folds of each design**: aggregate K-fold models by soft-prob averaging in probability space.
4. **Across designs**: ensemble diverse architectures by soft-prob or rank average with weights derived from OOF performance.
5. **Last resort**: stacking with a simple meta-learner, only when the above four yielded diminishing returns and OOF decorrelation still suggests headroom.

Snapshot ensembling and last-K averaging are alternatives to SWA at step 1, chosen based on schedule constraints.

## What Not to Stack

Not every combination is additive. A few interactions to avoid:

- **Snapshot ensembling + SWA**: both want to manipulate the LR schedule. Committing to cyclic restarts (snapshot) prevents the monotonic decay tail that SWA needs. Pick one.
- **Hard voting + soft voting in sequence**: once a member's output is thresholded to a discrete label, averaging it with soft probability maps is meaningless. Pick a single aggregation rule for a given layer.
- **Deep meta-learner on top of soft voting**: the soft-vote layer already produces a low-dimensional meta-feature space; a complex meta-learner overfits it trivially (see Principle 17 item 5).

## Checklist Before Deploying a Composed Ensemble

- Each layer's contribution has been measured in isolation (not only the final pipeline's score).
- OOF error correlations between members are tracked and members with correlation >0.95 are pruned.
- Inference cost has been budgeted against deployment constraints.
- The aggregation layer operates in probability space, not on thresholded masks (for dense prediction).
- The TTA set has been validated against a held-out metric, not assumed.
- Weights in any weighted aggregation step have been cross-validated to avoid OOF overfit.

## Diminishing Returns

Empirically, each successive ensemble layer yields less improvement than the previous one. A reasonable stopping rule: once the marginal gain from adding a new layer or member drops below the expected noise of the validation metric, stop adding and spend the engineering budget elsewhere (better base models, better data, better post-processing).
