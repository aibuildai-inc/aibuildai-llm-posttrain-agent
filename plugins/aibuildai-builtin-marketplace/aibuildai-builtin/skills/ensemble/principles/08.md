# Principle 8: Rank Averaging

**Space**: Prediction-space aggregation.

**Source**: MLWave *"Kaggle Ensembling Guide"*; general statistical practice for rank-based metrics.

## Definition

For each member's predictions, replace the raw scores by their **within-prediction ranks** (typically normalized to `[0, 1]` by dividing by the number of test samples). Average the rank vectors across members, and use the averaged rank as the final score. The argmax of averaged ranks is the final decision if a label is needed.

## Mechanism

Ranking is invariant to any monotonic transformation of the underlying scores. Two members that assign wildly different probability magnitudes but the same ordering of test samples produce identical rank vectors, so their calibration mismatch cancels out in the average. This makes rank averaging the natural aggregator for metrics that only depend on ordering: AUC, mean average precision, NDCG.

## Pros

- **Metric-preserving for rank-based metrics** — no information loss when only order matters.
- **Robust to calibration differences** across members. Mixing probabilities from a Transformer and a gradient-boosted tree works naturally without explicit temperature scaling.
- No need to calibrate individual members before combining.

## Cons

- **Destroys magnitude information.** Poor choice for log-loss, Brier score, or regression where actual score magnitudes matter.
- Ranks are defined per test set — adding or removing samples shifts all ranks, which breaks online or streaming inference use cases.
- Ties in original scores produce tied ranks; many tied ranks (e.g. models that clip probabilities at 0 and 1) distort the rank mass near the extremes.

## When to Use

When the evaluation metric is rank-based (AUC, MAP, NDCG) **and** member probability distributions disagree in scale or shape. Also useful as a robustness check: if soft voting and rank averaging give very different results, it is a signal that calibration differences between members matter and should be investigated.

## Common Misconception

That rank averaging is strictly better than probability averaging. It is better only when (a) the metric cares only about order or (b) calibration differences dominate signal differences. For log-loss, Brier, or regression targets, rank averaging discards exactly the information the metric rewards.

## Load-Bearing Quotes

> "Not all predictors are perfectly calibrated: they may be over- or underconfident when predicting a low or high probability… Therefore, first turn the predictions into ranks, then averaging these ranks."
>
> — MLWave *"Kaggle Ensembling Guide"*
