# Principle 2: Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)

**Space**: Weight-space. Produces a single model, not an ensemble.

**Source**: Izmailov et al. 2018, *"Averaging Weights Leads to Wider Optima and Better Generalization"* (arXiv:1803.05407); PyTorch `torch.optim.swa_utils`.

## Definition

Train the model normally for most of the run. In the **tail phase**, switch to a cyclical or high-constant learning rate and collect an equal-weight running average of parameter snapshots at regular intervals. At the end of training, replace the model weights with the SWA-averaged weights and re-estimate all batch-normalization running statistics with one extra forward pass over the training data.

## Mechanism

SGD with a small LR oscillates around a minimum rather than sitting still; each iterate is a sample from the neighborhood of a flat region. Averaging these iterates yields a parameter vector that sits more centrally in the flat region than any single iterate. "Flat" minima generalize better than "sharp" ones, so the averaged model usually outperforms the final SGD iterate on unseen data. Because BN running statistics depend on the weights, the averaged weights need their own BN-stat pass before deployment.

## Pros

- Produces a **single model**. Inference cost is one forward pass — identical to a non-ensembled baseline.
- Negligible training overhead (one BN-recalibration pass plus the per-step cost of maintaining a running average).
- Theoretically grounded in flat-minimum geometry.

## Cons

- Requires modifying the LR schedule in the tail phase (cyclical or high-constant). Cannot be bolted onto a standard cosine decay without changing the schedule.
- Requires BN-stat recalibration at the end. Skipping this step leaves the deployed model using training BN statistics from the non-averaged weights, which is inconsistent and often degrades accuracy.
- Benefits depend on SGD having entered the flat region before averaging begins. Averaging too early, while loss is still visibly descending, averages iterates from different regions and can hurt.

## When to Use

When a single deployable model is required (inference latency matters) but you still want generalization better than the final SGD iterate. SWA fits naturally into training pipelines that can tolerate a two-phase schedule (normal decay followed by a cyclical tail). In practice SWA is often the first weight-averaging method to try before escalating to snapshot ensembling.

## Common Misconception

That SWA is an ensemble of models. It is **not**. Izmailov et al. explicitly frame SWA as approximating the Fast Geometric Ensembling ensemble *with a single model*; PyTorch's implementation literally replaces the model weights with the running average at the end.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "SWA performs an equal average of the weights traversed by SGD with a modified learning rate schedule."
>
> — PyTorch blog post introducing `swa_utils`
