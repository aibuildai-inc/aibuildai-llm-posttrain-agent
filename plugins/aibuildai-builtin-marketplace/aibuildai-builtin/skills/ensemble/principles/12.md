# Principle 12: K-Fold CV Ensemble

**Space**: Data / pipeline.

**Source**: scikit-learn cross-validation documentation; general statistical practice.

## Definition

Partition the training data into **K equal, non-overlapping folds**. Train K models, each leaving one fold out as its validation set. Instead of discarding the K fold-models after computing their out-of-fold metric (the purely-validation use of K-fold), **keep them and average their predictions at inference**. This repurposes the cross-validation scaffold as an ensemble.

## Mechanism

Each of the K members has seen a different (K−1)/K subset of the training data, so the models disagree on samples near decision boundaries and agree on easy ones. Averaging their predictions reduces variance on the disagreement cases. The decorrelation is real but structurally weaker than bagging's bootstrap decorrelation because the fold splits are a fixed, non-random partition — different folds of the same dataset still share ≈80% of training samples when K=5.

## Two Distinct Uses of K-Fold

- **Validation-only**: train K models, compute per-fold metric, average the metric, then **discard the fold models** and retrain a single final model on all training data. The fold models are scaffolding; the validation metric measures expected test performance for a retrain-on-all model.
- **Ensemble**: keep the K fold models and use them at inference. Validation metric from CV is still reported but is now a pessimistic proxy for the ensemble's performance because each fold model is weaker than the K-way average.

Both can be done, or only one. The choice depends on whether a single retrain-on-all model beats the K-way ensemble (dataset-dependent).

## Pros

- **Zero training cost beyond running CV at all.** If CV is already being done for validation, the fold models exist already.
- Decorrelated members come for free — no manual diversification required.
- Produces a per-prediction uncertainty estimate (variance across folds) as a by-product.

## Cons

- Inference cost and storage multiply by K.
- Each member is trained on less data than a single retrain-on-all model would see. If the bias penalty of reduced training data exceeds the variance reduction from averaging, the ensemble can lose to a single retrained model — particularly on scarce data where every sample matters.
- For strong, low-variance learners (heavily regularized models), the K members are nearly identical and averaging provides little benefit while costing K× inference.

## When to Use

When the dataset is large enough that (K−1)/K samples is already sufficient to train a strong model (learning curve is flat there), when variance is the dominant error component, and when K× inference cost is affordable. The default K=5 works well in most settings; K=10 occasionally helps for very noisy metrics.

## Common Misconception

That K-fold ensemble is "just bagging." It is not. Bagging uses bootstrap resampling **with replacement** from the full training set, producing resamples of the same size N where each contains ≈63% unique rows. K-fold partitions the data **without replacement** into disjoint folds, producing exactly-(K−1)/K-sized training sets that share a fixed structured overlap. Bagging members have noisy mutual overlap; K-fold members have deterministic mutual overlap. In practice bagging produces more decorrelated members than K-fold at equal member count.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "KFold divides all the samples in k groups of samples, called folds, of equal sizes. The prediction function is learned using k - 1 folds, and the fold left out is used for test."
>
> — scikit-learn cross-validation documentation
