# Principle 1: Snapshot Ensembling

**Space**: Weight-space-origin, prediction-space aggregation at inference.

**Source**: Huang et al. 2017, *"Snapshot Ensembles: Train 1, get M for free"* (ICLR 2017, arXiv:1704.00109).

## Definition

A single training run uses a **cyclic cosine learning-rate schedule** that repeatedly drives the model to converge, restarts the LR to its initial value, and drives it to converge again. The weights at the end of each cycle are saved as separate checkpoints. At inference, each of the M saved checkpoints produces an independent prediction and the softmax (or sigmoid) outputs are averaged.

## Mechanism

The cyclic LR creates M distinct cycles inside one training run. At the end of each cycle the LR has annealed to nearly zero, so the model lands in a local minimum; the immediate restart to the initial LR kicks the weights into a different region of parameter space where a new descent begins. The result is M nearly-decorrelated networks from one wall-clock training budget. Inference is a standard prediction-space ensemble — it is *not* a weight-space method despite producing the snapshots from one run.

## Pros

- No additional training time compared to a plain single-run baseline (cycles share the total epoch budget).
- Diversity emerges "for free" from the LR restarts — no manual variation of seeds, data, or architecture is required.
- Trivial to add to any SGD pipeline that can accept a cyclic LR schedule.

## Cons

- Hard-coupled to a cyclic LR schedule. Bolting a "save every N epochs" rule onto an unmodified single-descent schedule produces M snapshots from inside the same basin and destroys the diversity premise.
- Inference cost and checkpoint storage both grow by M×.
- If total training epochs are held fixed, each cycle is shorter than a plain full run, so individual snapshots are less converged. With too many cycles the ensemble can lose to a single longer-run baseline.

## When to Use

When the training schedule can be redesigned from scratch and the M× inference overhead is acceptable. Typical M is 4–6. Avoid when the existing training code cannot tolerate an LR schedule change without re-validating end-to-end, or when every inference-side millisecond counts.

## Common Misconception

That snapshot ensembling is a *weight-space* method producing a single model. It is not. The M checkpoints remain separate at inference and are combined in prediction space. The related weight-space methods (SWA, last-K averaging, EMA) collapse the snapshots into one set of weights and cost only one forward pass at inference.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "The ensemble prediction at test time is the average of the last m (m≤M) model's softmax outputs."
>
> — Huang et al. 2017
