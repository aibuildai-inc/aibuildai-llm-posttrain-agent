# Principle 3: 90°/180°/270° Discrete Rotation (C4 / D4)

## Invariance it assumes

f(Rot_{90k}(x)) = f(x) for k ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}. Four-fold discrete rotational symmetry. If the augmentation set additionally includes transpose (diagonal mirror), the assumption becomes the full D4 dihedral group: eight orientation-equivalent views of any input.

## Mechanism

Pixel-exact rotation by multiples of 90° (no interpolation required); or diagonal transpose. Requires a square input shape, or tolerance for the H/W swap under 90° / 270°. Inverses are exact: Rot_90 ∘ Rot_270 = identity.

## When this invariance typically holds

- **Overhead remote sensing** explicitly normalized to have no preferred compass direction: many Earth-observation benchmarks, Mars / lunar surface imagery, intentionally azimuth-randomized aerial datasets.
- **Microscopy with no fixed specimen orientation**: cell cultures, histology patches whose slide orientation is arbitrary, electron microscopy of isotropic materials.
- **Natural textures**: grass patch, bark patch, sand, fabric weave.
- **Abstract 2D patterns**: isotropic synthetic data, certain physics simulations with rotational symmetry.

## When this invariance breaks

- **Any task with a gravity prior**: ground-level photos, standing subjects, outdoor scenes — same failure mode as vertical flip (Principle 2) compounded across 4 orientations.
- **Document and natural-scene text**: rotating a page 90° turns readable text into unreadable gibberish; the label (the text) is not preserved.
- **Character / digit recognition**: "6" ↔ "9" under 180°; most letters become non-letters under 90°.
- **Radiology with anatomical orientation**: chest X-ray has heart on the left, liver on the right, spine top-to-bottom — rotation destroys positional priors that the label depends on.
- **Depth-structured cross-sections**: seismic sediment slices, sonar profiles, bathymetry, soil cores. The vertical axis encodes depth; 90° rotation turns depth into lateral position.
- **Street-view, driving, dash-cam**: road, sky, buildings have fixed relative orientation.
- **Dermoscopy / retinal imaging**: standard acquisition protocol fixes orientation.

## Silent failure mode

Failure scales with how strong the orientation prior is. In extreme cases (OCR) training basically collapses. In moderate cases (natural photography) it looks like VFlip failure compounded across 4 orientations. Symptom: model learns a superposition of four unrelated orientation-specific features per class, each starved of data, instead of one strong feature.

## TTA use

8-way D4 TTA (or 4-way C4) is the canonical "paranoid" TTA for overhead imagery and isotropic microscopy. On domains that violate rotational invariance, D4 TTA is the single most destructive TTA commonly applied — it compounds four wrong views instead of one.

## Common misconception

"More TTA views is always better." No. Unsafe TTA compounds the signal violation. 8-way D4 TTA on a non-isotropic domain performs **worse** than no TTA, because seven of the eight views correspond to inputs the model was never trained on.
