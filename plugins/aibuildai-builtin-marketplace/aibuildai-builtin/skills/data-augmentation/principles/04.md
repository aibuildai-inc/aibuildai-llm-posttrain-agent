# Principle 4: Small-Angle Rotation

## Invariance it assumes

f(Rot_θ(x)) ≈ f(x) for |θ| ≤ ε for some small angle ε. The task is **locally** rotation-invariant within a bounded range, even if it is not globally invariant. Unlike the discrete C4 case (Principle 3), the decision here is not binary — it is about picking the right ε.

## Mechanism

Rotate the input (and spatial labels) by a small angle sampled uniformly in [−ε, +ε], typically 5°–15°. Requires interpolation (bilinear / bicubic) for pixel resampling and border-handling (reflect / replicate / constant) for the triangular regions revealed at the corners. Inverse is rotation by −θ.

## When this invariance typically holds for small ε

- **Natural-scene classification and detection**: small camera roll is plausible at acquisition; ≤10° augments the data realistically.
- **Medical imaging with mild scanner-angle variance**: chest X-ray, brain MRI axial slices — small angles approximate real patient-positioning variance.
- **Overhead remote sensing with arbitrary azimuth**: the rotation range can be the full ±180° (in which case Principle 3 supersedes).
- **Pathology / histology patches**: slide-on-stage rotation is arbitrary.

## When this invariance breaks — even for small ε

- **OCR / scene-text / handwriting**: real test-time text is within ±3° of horizontal; rotation above ~5° confuses the model because it will never see that much tilt at test.
- **Directional-label classification**: any task whose label depends on orientation — same failure as Principle 1 / Principle 2.
- **Thin-structure tasks** (vessel segmentation, crack detection, 1-pixel-wide features): interpolation during rotation blurs the thin structure, potentially breaking connectivity and losing the label.
- **Rigid calibrated imaging**: quantitative measurement with fixed acquisition geometry.

## Breakage as a function of ε

Unlike binary flips, rotation has a continuous knob. The question is "what is the largest ε for which the invariance still holds in this domain?" Rules of thumb derived from top Kaggle / published solutions:

- Generic natural images: ε ≈ 10°–15°.
- Medical images with fixed acquisition: ε ≈ 5°–10°.
- OCR / text: ε ≈ 2°–3° or disabled.
- Domains with a strong depth-axis prior and thin horizontal structure (sedimentary cross-sections, bathymetry): ε ≈ 0°–5°.
- Isotropic overhead: ε up to ±180° (in which case Principle 3 supersedes).

## Interaction with ShiftScaleRotate-style compounds

Albumentations `ShiftScaleRotate` draws rotation, shift, and scale independently. If rotation is unsafe, zeroing `rotate_limit` within such a compound leaves shift and scale active. This is a common pattern — rotation can be individually disabled inside a larger affine augmentation.

## Common misconception

"A small rotation is always a free gain." Not in domains where test-time orientation is tightly calibrated. In such domains, even a small ε teaches the model to expect variance that does not exist in the test distribution, and ceiling accuracy drops.
