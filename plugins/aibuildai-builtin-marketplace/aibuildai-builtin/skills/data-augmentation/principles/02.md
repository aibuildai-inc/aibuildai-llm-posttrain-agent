# Principle 2: Vertical Flip

## Invariance it assumes

f(VFlip(x)) = f(x). Predictions are unchanged when the image is flipped top-to-bottom. For spatial labels, masks / boxes / keypoints are mirrored along the horizontal axis in lock-step. The assumption fundamentally requires the **absence of a gravity-like reference frame** in the data.

## Mechanism

Mirror along the horizontal axis: output[r, c] = input[H-1-r, c]. Deterministic, no interpolation. Inverse is itself.

## When this invariance typically holds

- Data acquired with **no gravity prior in the image frame**: overhead satellite imagery stored in non-georeferenced orientation, top-down microscopy of cells on a slide before downstream spatial labelling.
- **Astronomical images** where up/down is a coordinate convention, not a physical direction.
- **Abstract 2D textures or patterns** with no preferred direction: seamless materials, certain synthetic data.
- **Isotropic overhead remote sensing** (some Mars-surface, lunar-surface datasets, intentionally-normalized aerial).

## When this invariance breaks

- **Ground-level outdoor or indoor photography**: gravity dictates sky / ceiling up, ground / floor down. VFlip produces inputs the model will never see at inference.
- **Street / driving / dash-cam imagery**: road below, sky above.
- **Natural-scene photographs of people, animals, vehicles** — all have a gravity-defined top and bottom.
- **Ultrasound abdominal imaging**: the probe surface is always on one side by display convention.
- **Radiology with fixed patient orientation**: chest X-ray has lungs above diaphragm; brain MRI axial slices have nose up.
- **Depth-axis-preserving cross-sections**: seismic sediment slices (shallow layers near top, deep layers near bottom), sonar depth profiles, bathymetric charts, soil-core stratigraphy, geological cross-sections. The vertical axis encodes depth or geological age; flipping it inverts the depth ordering.
- **Satellite imagery in geo-referenced orientation**: north-up convention.
- **Anything with a visible horizon**.

## Silent failure mode

Same mechanism as HFlip but typically **larger in magnitude** — gravity priors tend to be stronger than lateral-asymmetry priors. The model wastes capacity learning "upside-down" patterns that never occur at test; ceiling accuracy drops by several tenths of a point to a few points.

## TTA use

Safe iff training-time VFlip is safe on this task, which is rare in practice. Adding VFlip TTA on top of HFlip TTA does not automatically work — the two test different invariances and must pass their check independently.

## Common misconception

"If HFlip works, VFlip probably works too." No. HFlip tests lateral label symmetry; VFlip tests the absence of a gravity-like reference frame. Many datasets satisfy the first and violate the second. The two are independent checks.
