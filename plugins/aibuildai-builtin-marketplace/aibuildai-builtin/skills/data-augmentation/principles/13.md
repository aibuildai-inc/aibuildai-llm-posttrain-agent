# Principle 13: Additive Gaussian Noise

## Invariance it assumes

f(x + ε) = f(x) for ε ~ N(0, σ²). The task is robust to small additive Gaussian perturbation of pixel values, approximating test-time sensor noise.

## Mechanism

For each training sample, sample an independent Gaussian per pixel with standard deviation σ, add to the input, optionally clip to the input's dynamic range. Typical σ in normalized-pixel units ≈ 0.01–0.05.

## When this invariance typically holds

- **Low-SNR consumer photography**: test-time images will have camera-sensor noise approximating Gaussian, especially at high ISO.
- **Standard RGB classification** where a small amount of acquisition noise is realistic.
- **Intentional regularization**: even when test images are noise-free, small training-time noise can serve as a mild regularizer in the "jittered input" sense.
- **Simulation-to-real transfer**: synthetic noise bridges the gap between clean-render training data and noisy real-world test data.

## When this invariance breaks

- **Data with a different physical noise model**:
  - Speckle noise in ultrasound (multiplicative, non-Gaussian).
  - Salt-and-pepper noise in some sensors.
  - Poisson shot noise in photon-counting detectors (X-ray, low-light imaging).
  - Coherent-imaging noise in SAR and seismic reflection data (correlated across pixels, not i.i.d. Gaussian).
- Training against the wrong noise distribution teaches the model to tolerate noise it will never encounter, and fails to tolerate the noise it will.
- **Scientific imaging where acquired noise has been physically characterized and possibly corrected**: adding synthetic Gaussian on top is double-counting.
- **Tasks relying on precise pixel values**: quantitative imaging measurements.

## When noise is essentially free

If the sensor noise model is already approximately Gaussian **and** the augmentation σ is below the sensor noise floor, the extra noise acts as mild regularization and is harmless in either direction. This is the regime in which Gaussian-noise augmentation is a "free" additional regularizer.

## Interaction with image statistics

Gaussian noise shifts neither the mean nor the long-run histogram of the training set (it has zero mean), so it does not require normalization-stat recomputation. This is one of the reasons it is popular as a default, even in domains where a more physically correct noise model would be preferable.

## Common misconception

"Noise augmentation is always safe regularization." Only when the synthetic noise distribution matches the sensor noise distribution, or when the augmentation σ is well below the existing noise floor. Adding Gaussian noise to a domain with physically baked-in non-Gaussian noise — or to calibrated measurement data — is an active distortion that teaches the model the wrong robustness.
