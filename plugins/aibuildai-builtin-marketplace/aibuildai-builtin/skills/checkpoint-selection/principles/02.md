# Principle 02: Last-N checkpoint weight averaging

**Layer**: Layer 1 weight-space. Produces a single deployable checkpoint per fold.

**Source**: PyTorch `torch.optim.swa_utils` documentation; timm `avg_checkpoints.py`; classical Polyak-Ruppert averaging.

## Definition

Save checkpoints at the last N evaluation points of a normal training run (any LR schedule), then element-wise average their parameter tensors into a single deployed model. No LR schedule modification, no in-training bookkeeping, no retraining — purely a post-hoc operation on already-saved checkpoints. Recommended N is 5 to 10; checkpoints should be spaced at least one epoch apart.

## Mechanism

Any training run that saves checkpoints at regular intervals already holds K parameter vectors. Averaging them element-wise produces a new model whose weights are the arithmetic mean of the K. The resulting model benefits when the last K checkpoints were collected after the LR had decayed enough that the weights were no longer moving rapidly — i.e., during the tail of a decay schedule, where iterates wander around a flat region. The arithmetic mean then sits closer to the flat-minimum centre than any single iterate, which generalizes better.

## Pros

- Zero training modification. Works on any existing training script with checkpoint saving.
- Single-model inference cost.
- Drop-in retroactive improvement — an already-completed training run can be upgraded by this step alone.

## Cons

- Quality is sensitive to which N checkpoints (spacing, training phase). Averaging across radically different training stages harms the result.
- No theoretical guarantee of flat-minimum geometry — unlike SWA, the LR schedule is not designed to keep iterates in a flat region.
- BatchNorm running statistics may be stale; recalibrate via one forward pass over training data (`torch.optim.swa_utils.update_bn`).

## When to Use

When an existing training run is complete or the LR schedule cannot be modified, and a free post-hoc accuracy upgrade is worth the few minutes of compute. Commonly used as the baseline before deciding whether to invest in proper SWA with a modified schedule.

## Common Misconception

That last-N averaging is identical to SWA. PyTorch documentation is explicit that it is not: SWA additionally requires a modified cyclical or high-constant LR schedule and BN-stat re-estimation. Plain last-N averaging does neither. Last-N is the schedule-agnostic, effort-minimal cousin of SWA, not a synonym.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "Averaged SGD is often employed in conjunction with a decaying learning rate. By contrast, SWA is focused on an equal average of SGD iterates with a modified cyclical or high constant learning rate."
>
> — PyTorch `swa_utils` documentation
