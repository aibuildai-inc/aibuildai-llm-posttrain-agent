# Principle 08: Multi-strategy combo within one fold (best + SWA + EMA)

**Layer**: Layer 2 prediction-space.

**Source**: PyTorch `torch.optim.swa_utils` documentation (combined SWA + EMA recipes); Kaggle TGS Salt 1st place writeup (combining weight-space and prediction-space techniques in one pipeline).

## Definition

During one fold's training, save THREE checkpoints in parallel: (1) the best-validation working-`θ` (Principle 01), (2) the SWA-averaged checkpoint after the SWA-LR phase (Principle 04), and (3) the EMA shadow checkpoint at training end (Principle 05). At inference, predict with all three checkpoints separately, average the output probabilities. When training compute does not permit the SWA-LR phase, fall back to a 2-way combo: best-val + EMA only.

## Mechanism

The three checkpoints capture orthogonal aspects of the optimization trajectory: the best-val checkpoint is the single point with peak validation; the SWA average is the centre of the flat region around the convergence basin; the EMA shadow is the low-pass-filtered trajectory mean. Their predictions disagree non-trivially — the disagreement satisfies the ambiguity-decomposition diversity condition (Principle 07) and the ensemble error drops below the best member error. Documented gains are typically 0.5–2% over the best single member.

## Pros

- Stronger than any single Layer 1 rule across most tasks.
- Cost: 1× training (with SWA phase) + 3× inference per fold — the inference overhead is usually negligible compared to training.
- All three components are first-class supported in PyTorch (`AveragedModel` for SWA and EMA via custom avg_fn).

## Cons

- Requires the training script to maintain SWA and EMA streams in parallel — additional memory equal to twice the model size.
- Inference script must load three artifacts and align ID orders.
- Requires BatchNorm re-estimation for the SWA and EMA branches (one extra forward pass each).

## When to Use

As the recommended default for our pipeline when training compute permits the SWA-LR phase. When SWA is too expensive (very small datasets where even 25% extra compute is a meaningful fraction), drop to best + EMA — this 2-way combo is still strictly better than single-best.

## Common Misconception

That combining SWA and EMA double-counts the same flat-minimum bias. They behave differently in practice: SWA averages the SGD trajectory at SWA-LR; EMA low-pass-filters the entire trajectory at the standard LR. Their predictions disagree by enough that the average outperforms either alone in most documented benchmarks.
