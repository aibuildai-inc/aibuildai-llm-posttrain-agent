# Principle 06: Snapshot Ensemble (cyclic LR)

**Layer**: Layer 1 weight-space (when the M cycle-end checkpoints are reduced to one model) or Layer 2 prediction-space (when each cycle-end is kept separately and predictions are averaged).

**Source**: Huang, Li, Pleiss, Liu, Hopcroft, Weinberger 2017, "Snapshot Ensembles: Train 1, get M for free", arXiv:1704.00109; Loshchilov & Hutter 2017, "SGDR: Stochastic Gradient Descent with Warm Restarts", arXiv:1608.03983.

## Definition

Train with a cosine-annealed LR schedule restarted every `T` epochs (cosine annealing with warm restarts, SGDR). After each cycle of `T` epochs, save the cycle-end checkpoint. After M cycles, you have M snapshots collected from one training run. Use the M snapshots either as a prediction ensemble (average their predictions at inference) or as options for downstream selection (Caruana, Principle 11).

## Mechanism

The cosine-annealed LR within a cycle drives the iterate toward a local optimum; the warm restart at the start of the next cycle injects enough energy to escape that basin and explore another region of the loss landscape. Each cycle-end thus sits in a different local minimum. The M snapshots are sufficiently diverse — they make different mistakes — so prediction averaging benefits from the Krogh-Vedelsby ambiguity decomposition (ensemble error = mean member error − member disagreement).

## Pros

- M models for the cost of one training run, given M cycles fit in the same wall-clock budget as a single training without restarts.
- Diversity is automatic — different basins guarantee non-trivial disagreement, no special diversity-encouragement loss is needed.
- Compatible with any architecture; cosine-with-restarts is a drop-in LR scheduler in PyTorch (`torch.optim.lr_scheduler.CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts`).

## Cons

- Cycle count and length are extra hyperparameters; cycles too short → not converged; cycles too long → less diversity.
- M× inference cost when used as a prediction ensemble.
- Requires the warm-restart LR shape; cannot be added retroactively to a completed training run.

## When to Use

When training compute is the binding constraint and you would otherwise train M independent models for an ensemble. Snapshot ensemble extracts comparable diversity from one training run. Particularly effective for image segmentation and dense-prediction tasks where individual models converge slowly and cycle-ends differ meaningfully in their predictions.

## Common Misconception

That snapshots can be weight-averaged like last-N or top-K. They CANNOT — the cycle-ends sit in DIFFERENT basins by construction (that is the whole point of warm restarts), so weight-averaging across snapshots produces an interpolated point in a non-convex region between basins, which is worse than any individual snapshot. Only PREDICTION averaging is correct for snapshot ensembles.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "We propose to train a single neural network, converging to several local minima along its optimization path and saving the model parameters at each. We use the resulting snapshots as ensemble members. By cycling the learning rate using a warm restart schedule, we encourage the model to escape from local minima and converge to several different ones."
>
> — Huang et al. 2017, arXiv:1704.00109, abstract
