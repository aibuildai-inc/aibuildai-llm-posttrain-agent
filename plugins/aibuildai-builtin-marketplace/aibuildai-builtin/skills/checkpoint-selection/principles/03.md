# Principle 03: Top-K checkpoint weight averaging

**Layer**: Layer 1 weight-space. Produces a single deployable checkpoint per fold.

**Source**: ChemProp documentation, checkpoint averaging options (`avg5`, `avg_many`); timm `avg_checkpoints.py`.

## Definition

Collect K checkpoints whose validation scores are the K highest seen during training (not the most recent K — that is Principle 02). Element-wise average their parameter tensors into a single deployed model. Recommended K is 5; require minimum spacing of one epoch between selected members so that K near-identical iterates from a single spike are not all selected.

## Mechanism

Top-K averaging trades off two effects against each other. On the upside, by definition the K members are higher-validation than a random selection from the trajectory, so the average inherits some of that elevated quality. On the downside, top-K is itself a selection set with multiple-comparison inflation: each of the K is a noisy `argmax` over a sub-window, and averaging only partially undoes the selection bias. The net effect is typically positive when training has converged (so high-validation iterates sit in the same flat region) and negative when training is still moving rapidly (top-K members are then drawn from disparate basins, and weight-averaging across basins is meaningless).

## Pros

- Typically outperforms last-N averaging when the LR schedule was not tuned for tail flatness — high-validation iterates have at least passed the validation filter, while last-N may include a noisy run-end iterate.
- Single-model inference cost; same retroactive applicability as last-N.

## Cons

- Top-K members may all sit near the same noisy spike if the val curve has a single peak; require minimum-spacing to mitigate.
- Same BN stale-statistics issue as Principle 02; refresh via `update_bn`.
- Over-selects high-validation iterates that may be overfit to val noise — the averaging only partially repairs this.

## When to Use

When a training run has saved checkpoints at every evaluation epoch and the val curve shows non-trivial fluctuation (multiple peaks rather than monotone improvement). The minimum-spacing requirement disqualifies this rule when only the last few iterates are saved. Use `K = 5` as the default; ChemProp's `avg5` is a production reference.

## Common Misconception

That averaging the top K of all checkpoints saved during training is always better than averaging the last N. It is not. When training has not converged, top-K spans different basins and the average is incoherent; when training has converged, last-N members all sit in the converged region and behave like a Polyak-Ruppert average. Choose top-K specifically when you have evidence the curve fluctuates around a converged plateau.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "`avg5`: average the parameters of the 5 model checkpoints with the lowest validation loss."
>
> — ChemProp documentation, checkpoint averaging options
