# Principle 05: Exponential Moving Average of weights (EMA)

**Layer**: Layer 1 weight-space. Produces a single deployable shadow model maintained alongside the optimizer.

**Source**: timm `model_ema.py`; YOLOv5 `utils/torch_utils.py:ModelEMA`; Tarvainen & Valpola 2017, "Mean teachers are better role models: Weight-averaged consistency targets improve semi-supervised deep learning results", arXiv:1703.01780; PyTorch `torch.optim.swa_utils.AveragedModel(avg_fn=...)` with EMA update function.

## Definition

Maintain a "shadow" parameter tensor `θ_ema` alongside the optimizer's working `θ`. After every optimizer step, update the shadow via `θ_ema ← decay · θ_ema + (1 − decay) · θ`. Typical `decay = 0.9999` for very long trainings, `0.999` or `0.998` for shorter runs. Deploy `θ_ema` (not `θ`) at inference. Re-estimate BatchNorm running statistics on the EMA model before deployment.

## Mechanism

EMA is a low-pass filter on the optimizer trajectory in parameter space. Equivalently, it is an infinite-window weighted average with exponentially decaying weights, with effective window length `~1/(1-decay)` steps. The EMA shadow lags the working weights, smoothing out the high-frequency oscillations that come from mini-batch noise and LR fluctuations. At deployment, this lag has the same flat-minimum advantage as SWA but does not require an LR-schedule change.

## Pros

- Cheap: one extra weight tensor per model, one extra elementwise op per step.
- Schedule-agnostic: works with cosine decay, linear warmup-decay, or any other schedule.
- Widely used in production: vision transformers (DeiT, BEiT), object detection (YOLO family), diffusion models — typical 0.5–1.5% accuracy gain.
- Implementable in three lines of PyTorch via `torch.optim.swa_utils.AveragedModel(model, avg_fn=lambda avg, x, n: decay*avg + (1-decay)*x)`.

## Cons

- The shadow lags the working weights — needs at least a few thousand steps before the EMA is meaningful; deploying EMA from too-early in training is worse than deploying the working weights.
- BatchNorm re-estimation is recommended (same caveat as SWA).
- The decay constant is yet another hyperparameter; typical defaults (0.9999) work but may need tuning for very short or very long runs.

## When to Use

As the lowest-friction Layer 1 rule: any training script can have EMA added in five lines without changing the LR schedule. Pair with Principle 01 (best-val checkpoint of the working `θ`) and average their predictions at inference (Principle 08, multi-strategy combo) for the recommended-default in our pipeline.

## Common Misconception

That EMA needs the SWA-LR schedule. It does not — EMA works with any schedule, including the default cosine decay; that is precisely why it is cheaper to adopt than SWA. The two methods are complementary, not interchangeable.
