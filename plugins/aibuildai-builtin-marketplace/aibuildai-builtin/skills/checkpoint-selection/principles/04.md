# Principle 04: Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)

**Layer**: Layer 1 weight-space. Produces a single deployable checkpoint per fold.

**Source**: Izmailov, Podoprikhin, Garipov, Vetrov, Wilson 2018, "Averaging Weights Leads to Wider Optima and Better Generalization", arXiv:1803.05407; PyTorch `torch.optim.swa_utils.SWALR`, `AveragedModel`, `update_bn`.

## Definition

Train normally to a near-converged state. Then switch the LR schedule to a high-constant or cyclical SWA-LR (typically 0.05 of peak LR for a constant phase, or a triangular schedule that periodically returns to a moderately high value). During the SWA phase, equal-average all SGD iterates collected at every epoch end into the running model. Re-estimate BatchNorm running statistics at the end via one forward pass over the training data. Deploy the SWA-averaged model.

## Mechanism

The SWA-LR phase walks the iterate around a flat region of the loss landscape (the high-constant or cyclical LR is large enough to keep the iterate moving rather than collapsing to a point, but not so large that it leaves the basin). The arithmetic mean of these iterates sits closer to the centre of the flat region than any individual iterate. Wider, flatter optima are known to generalize better than sharp ones, so the SWA mean typically outperforms the SGD endpoint.

## Pros

- Documented 1–2% top-1 accuracy improvement on standard image-classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet) over SGD endpoints.
- Single-model inference cost.
- First-class support in PyTorch (`torch.optim.swa_utils`) and timm.

## Cons

- Requires modifying the LR schedule, which can disrupt other carefully tuned training-loop hyperparameters.
- BatchNorm running statistics MUST be re-estimated; skipping this step typically wipes out the gain.
- The SWA-LR phase length is an extra hyperparameter (usually 25% of total training).

## When to Use

When training compute permits at least 25% additional epochs for the SWA phase, and BatchNorm is the dominant normalization layer in the model. For LayerNorm-based models (most transformers), SWA still helps but the BN-update step is unnecessary. For tabular GBDTs, SWA does not apply (no continuous parameter trajectory).

## Common Misconception

That SWA is the same as Polyak-Ruppert / last-N averaging (Principle 02). It is not — SWA prescribes the LR schedule (high-constant or cyclical SWA-LR) AND the BN re-estimation step, neither of which Principle 02 requires. The PyTorch documentation is explicit about the distinction.
