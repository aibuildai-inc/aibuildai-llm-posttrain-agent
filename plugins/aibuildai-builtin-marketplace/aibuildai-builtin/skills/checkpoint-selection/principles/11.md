# Principle 11: Caruana ensemble selection (forward search)

**Layer**: Layer 3 across-attempt advanced. Generalizes Principle 10 from static top-K to data-driven forward selection.

**Source**: Caruana, Niculescu-Mizil, Crew, Ksikes 2004, "Ensemble Selection from Libraries of Models", ICML; widely used in Kaggle ensembling pipelines.

## Definition

Instead of fixing K and taking top-K (Principle 10), perform forward greedy selection on the OOF set:
1. Start with the empty ensemble.
2. At each step, evaluate every available attempt: tentatively add it to the current ensemble (with replacement allowed — i.e., a member can be selected multiple times), recompute the ensemble OOF metric.
3. Choose the attempt whose addition most reduces ensemble OOF error; commit that addition.
4. Stop when no addition reduces OOF error or when a max-size cap (typically 50 selections) is reached.
5. The output is a (possibly non-uniform) weighted average of the selected attempts; the weight of each attempt equals the count of times it was selected divided by total selections.

## Mechanism

Caruana selection directly optimizes the OOF metric over ensemble weights, but only over the discrete simplex of "include/exclude with replacement counts". The hypothesis space is much smaller than free weighted averaging, so OOF-overfitting is bounded — empirically, the gap between OOF-selected and held-out ensemble error is small. The greedy with-replacement step has O(N²) cost per addition (evaluate N attempts × averaging cost), totally O(N²·max_selections) for the whole procedure.

## Pros

- Typically Pareto-dominates static top-K when N ≥ 20 — the data-driven weighting picks better than uniform top-K.
- Handles correlated members gracefully — low-quality members correlated with strong members are not selected, even if their individual scores are middling.
- Produces interpretable weights (count of times each member was selected), which can be inspected for sanity.
- Robust to large libraries with many low-quality members.

## Cons

- Requires a per-sample OOF matrix in memory at aggregation time (N × n_samples × n_classes for classification).
- Can over-pack a few strong members ("over-selection"); the typical 50-cap mitigates but caps the diversity.
- Greedy is not globally optimal; a true backward-elimination or beam-search would be better but more expensive.

## When to Use

As a drop-in replacement for Principle 10 when N ≥ 20 and the OOF set is large enough (n ≥ 1000) that the OOF metric is statistically meaningful. For small N or small OOF, static top-K (Principle 10) is more conservative and generally safer.

## Common Misconception

That Caruana selection is the same as stacking. It is not — stacking trains a meta-learner (e.g., logistic regression, LightGBM) on the OOF predictions as features, and the meta-learner has free weights subject to its own hypothesis space. Caruana selection only chooses a discrete weighted combination from the existing model library, with no learned meta-layer. The hypothesis space is far smaller and far less prone to overfitting; that is precisely why Caruana selection is "free" in expectation while stacking can hurt private-LB.

The selection procedure uses forward stepwise selection from the library and is robust to overfitting through bagging and selection without replacement up to a fixed library size."
>
