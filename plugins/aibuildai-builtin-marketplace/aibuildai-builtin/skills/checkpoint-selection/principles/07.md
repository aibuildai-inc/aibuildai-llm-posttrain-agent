# Principle 07: Top-K checkpoint prediction averaging

**Layer**: Layer 2 prediction-space. K models at inference, one prediction tensor per model, averaged at the output layer.

**Source**: Caruana, Niculescu-Mizil, Crew, Ksikes 2004, "Ensemble Selection from Libraries of Models", ICML; Kaggle TGS Salt 1st place writeup; Krogh & Vedelsby 1995, "Neural Network Ensembles, Cross Validation, and Active Learning", NIPS 7 (the ambiguity decomposition).

## Definition

Identify the K checkpoints with the highest validation scores during training (the same K that Principle 03 would *weight*-average). Keep them as separate model files. At inference, predict with each, average the output probabilities (or rank-average the logits when output scales differ across checkpoints). Recommended K = 5; require minimum spacing of one epoch between selected members.

## Mechanism

Prediction-space averaging is loss-decomposable via the Krogh-Vedelsby ambiguity decomposition: `ensemble_error = mean(member_error) − mean(member_disagreement)`. Diversity (the disagreement term) is the load-bearing property — combining N members that make the same mistakes gives no improvement, while combining N members that make decorrelated mistakes can dramatically reduce error. Top-K checkpoints from one training run typically have non-trivial diversity because the validation curve fluctuates around different points in parameter space.

## Pros

- Typically beats both single-best and weight-averaging when the K checkpoints come from different basins (e.g., snapshot ensemble — see Principle 06).
- Works for any model whose training has been completed; needs no schedule modification.
- Calibration is preserved: averaging probabilities is a valid probabilistic operation.

## Cons

- K× inference cost.
- Alignment of input ID order across the K predictions is the responsibility of the inference script; mis-alignment silently destroys the gain.
- Memory: must hold K model copies in inference RAM/VRAM, or stream-load one at a time.

## When to Use

When K = 3–10 cycle-ends are available from a snapshot-ensemble training (Principle 06), or when training has produced multiple high-validation iterates from disparate epochs (typical of cosine-with-restarts schedules). Pair with Principle 08 (multi-strategy combo) when both best-val and SWA/EMA are available.

## Common Misconception

That top-K weight averaging (Principle 03) and top-K prediction averaging (this principle) are interchangeable. They differ — weight averaging only works when the K checkpoints sit in the same basin (same connected sublevel set of the loss landscape); prediction averaging works regardless of basin. When you do not know whether the K members are in the same basin, prefer prediction averaging.

## Load-Bearing Quote

> "Ensemble selection's library is a collection of models that may differ in their hyperparameters, learning algorithms, or training data. Selection finds a subset whose averaged predictions perform well on a held-out validation set. The diversity of the library is more important than the quality of any single model."
>
> — Caruana et al. 2004, "Ensemble Selection from Libraries of Models", ICML
